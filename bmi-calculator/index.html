What is BMI?

Body mass index is weight divided by height squared (kg/m²). The World Health Organization uses it as a quick population-level screening measure: under 18.5 is classed as underweight, 18.5–24.9 as a healthy range, 25–29.9 as overweight and 30+ as obese.

FAQ

Is BMI accurate for everyone?

No — it's a screening tool, not a diagnosis. It can't tell muscle from fat, so muscular athletes often score "overweight" while being very lean, and it can understate body fat in older adults. Waist circumference and body-fat percentage give a fuller picture. For any health decision, talk to a medical professional.

How is BMI calculated in imperial units?

BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)². The 703 factor converts the result to the same kg/m² scale as the metric formula.

What does the healthy weight range mean?

It's the weight span that would put your BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 at your height — a reference range, not a personal target. Individual healthy weights vary with build, age and muscle mass.

Is my data private?

Completely. The calculation runs in your browser on a static page; your height and weight are never transmitted or stored.
