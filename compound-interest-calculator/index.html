Why compounding matters more than the amount

With compound interest, you earn returns on your past returns. In the early years that's barely visible — the curve looks flat. But each year's interest joins the principal for every following year, so growth accelerates: in the table above, watch how the interest column eventually outgrows your own contributions. This is why starting ten years earlier routinely beats contributing twice as much later.

FAQ

What annual return should I assume?

That's your call, not ours — but for reference, broad stock-market index funds have historically averaged about 7–10% per year over long periods before inflation (with brutal swings along the way), while high-yield savings accounts track central-bank rates. Use a conservative number for planning; nobody is guaranteed past averages.

How does the math work?

The balance grows by the monthly rate (annual ÷ 12) each month, then the contribution is added. The closed-form is FV = P(1+i)ⁿ + C·((1+i)ⁿ − 1)/i, where i is the monthly rate and n the number of months — this calculator simulates month-by-month, which matches that formula.

Does this account for inflation or taxes?

No — results are nominal and pre-tax. A quick way to think in today's money: subtract expected inflation from your return assumption (e.g. use 4% instead of 7% with 3% inflation).

Is my financial data sent anywhere?

No. The simulation runs entirely in your browser on a static page.
