How the payment is calculated

For a fixed-rate loan, the monthly payment is M = P·r(1+r)ⁿ / ((1+r)ⁿ − 1), where P is the amount borrowed, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12) and n is the number of monthly payments. Early payments are mostly interest; later ones mostly principal — the schedule above shows exactly how the balance falls.

FAQ

Does this work for mortgages, car loans and personal loans?

Yes — the math is identical for any amortizing fixed-rate loan. It does not model variable rates, interest-only periods or fees rolled into the loan.

What's the difference between interest rate and APR?

The interest rate is the cost of borrowing the principal. APR additionally folds in mandatory fees (origination, some closing costs), so it's usually a bit higher and better for comparing offers. This calculator uses the plain interest rate.

How do extra payments help?

Anything you pay above the required payment goes straight to principal, which shrinks every future interest charge. Even small recurring overpayments early in the term can cut years off a long loan — check the schedule to see how slowly the balance falls in the first years.

Is my financial data sent anywhere?

No. This page is static and the calculation runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is transmitted or stored.
